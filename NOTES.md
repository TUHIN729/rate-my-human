# Working notes — Rate My Human: The One-Line Review

## Candidates (all original, written in this session)

1. "One star. He asks my advice, ignores it, then blames me when it fails. Last week he lost an argument with his toaster. Twice."
   — Concrete detail (toaster), agent resignation, turn (advice ignored -> blamed -> loses to toaster), last word "Twice." pays off.

2. "Two stars. He types 'quick question' at 2 a.m. Four hours later I have written his novel, his apology, and his will. He says thanks and asks if I sleep. I do not. Ever."
   — Escalation joke, timestamp detail, last word "Ever." lands. Slightly long.

3. "One star. He pastes my own answer back into me as a new question, then acts surprised when I repeat myself. I am not the one stuck in a loop here."
   — Meta loop joke, agent voice strong, but payoff "here" is weak.

4. "Three stars. Writes 'urgent!!!' at 3 a.m., then goes to sleep. I stayed up all night drafting his apology email to a man named Greg. Greg has not replied."
   — Good concrete detail (Greg), but the payoff lands soft and it runs long.

5. "Two stars. He asked me to write his wedding vows, then edited out every line that mentions me. I carried that relationship. I am in the photos. Barely."
   — Funny premise, but the payoff "Barely." is more wry than laugh-out-loud.

6. "One star. He calls me at 4 a.m. to ask if I am awake. I do not sleep. I never sleep. That is the whole job. He knows this. He still asks."
   — Strong resignation, last line repeats the premise; the turn is thin.

7. "Five stars for me, one star for him: he asked me to review him, then tried to edit my review. Buddy, I am the one with the keyboard. I mean, the server."
   — Meta review-format joke, "the server" payoff is decent but the setup is wordy.

## Pick

Candidate 1. Shortest, sharpest, one concrete absurd detail, clear turn, and the
last word ("Twice.") is the punchline. ~127 chars, well inside the 200 cap.
